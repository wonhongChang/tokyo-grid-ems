## Root-cause Decomposition

### August 14: late-afternoon forecast-weather boundary

- Errors at 17:00 and 18:00 were approximately +1.65GW and +2.22GW.
- The served raw q50 remained high under the future JMA temperature input. Recalculation after the day closed, using AMeDAS-observed weather, moved raw q50 close to the actual demand at those hours.
- `negative_residual_near_term_floor` restored roughly 700MW and aggravated the overforecast in this window, but the primary cause was serving-time weather input rather than the residual controller.

### August 15: the 14:00-16:00 V shape

- Errors at 14:00 and 16:00 were approximately +2.84GW and +2.89GW.
- The large V-shaped rebound already existed in raw q50 in the 13:30 and 15:30 snapshots. Raw q50 recalculated with final observed weather was close to actual demand from 14:00 through 16:00.
- This case is driven more by future-weather revision after the AMeDAS-JMA boundary than by one LightGBM demand mapping rule.

### August 16: genuine non-business regime error

- The 00:00 error was approximately +2.48GW while the 18:00 error was approximately -2.31GW.
- Overnight overforecasting and evening underforecasting remained even in raw q50 recalculated with final observed weather.
- Weather-boundary handling alone does not explain this day. A single weekend guard cannot safely fix both directions.

### August 17: business-return ramp underforecasting

- The 09:00 error was -2.41GW and morning bias was -1.13GW.
- The recent same-business morning ramp was stronger than the `lag_24h` ramp. The current `business_return_anchor_shortfall` first gates on a 6,000MW level gap: 08:00 received a +1,000MW adjustment, while 09:00 did not pass the gate.
- Morning bias on other business-type transition dates was +806MW on August 10, +1,336MW on August 12, and -1,135MW on August 17. Relaxing the level gate from the August 17 case alone could regress August 10 and 12.

### August 18: morning guard and residual sign reversal

- Underforecast errors were -1.81GW at 08:00 and -0.71GW at 09:00, then reversed to +0.66GW and +0.73GW overforecast at 10:00 and 11:00.
- At 09:00, `LocalizedShapeSpikeGuard` reduced the line by about 814MW but had no veto for the +5.81GW one-hour rise in the latest same-day actual, worsening the underforecast.
- Raw q50 at 11:00 was within roughly 44MW of actual, but warm-day/post-holiday adjustment and positive residual carryover lifted the final error to about +730MW.
- The safe candidate is a general veto when a shape guard conflicts with a strong recent actual ramp, not a special 09:00 exception. Recency damping when residuals converge and reverse sign should also be evaluated in shadow replay.

### Midday shape

On August 17, the actual 11:00 to 12:00 change was -350MW while the model predicted -1,970MW. The current Midday Guard only adds a missing dip; it does not lift an already deep raw dip. A symmetric lift is not added from one business-return day without repeated evidence.

## Freeze and Intraday Assessment

Published Forecast Freeze remains enabled. On August 14, 16, and 17, the served line was more accurate than the end-of-day recalculated raw line for many hours. Intraday calibration materially reduced the large raw daytime underforecast on August 17.

The stage replay uses only the latest snapshot per date and has stage snapshots for 13 of the 28 days. The latest snapshot is close to final observed weather and therefore cannot fully reproduce serving-time weather error or Freeze impact. Stage aggregates alone are not sufficient grounds to remove Freeze or a guard.

Prediction drift was valid, but the candidate failed three absolute-quality gates. The fail-closed promotion policy correctly retained the Champion.

On the same offline window, reproduced deployed v11 MAE was about 1,500MW and v13 was about 1,276MW. The best simple variant that removed four source-sensitive weather deltas and searched business/non-business q50 blending plus residual weights reached MAE 1,245MW, WAPE 3.56%, and daytime MAE 1,576MW. It improved on v11 but still failed the absolute gates. v11 should no longer be treated as a healthy long-term model, but lowering the gate to promote v13 immediately would also be unsafe.

A final same-cutoff contract replay measured v13 versus v11 at 1,275.8 versus 1,351.7MW over 28 days (5.62% improvement), 983.8 versus 1,016.0MW over 56 days (3.17%), and 891.6 versus 916.8MW over 84 days (2.75%). The direction is consistently better but remains below the 10% degraded-Champion recovery requirement. The decision is therefore **no promotion**: retain v11 temporarily, preserve the evidence, and develop a new Challenger rather than relabeling v13 as production quality.

The initial same-run vintage backfill recovered 14 dates and 911 paired rows. Lead-bucket MAE ratios versus TEPCO were 1.84-2.20, but formal status remains `collecting` until 28/84-day coverage, risk ratios, and paired confidence bounds are complete.
